§ 3.0  ·  the pipeline

Eight stages, two flows, and 24 numbers between them

The chapter's eight stages are not a sequence. Stages 1, 2 and 4 are a shared foundation — pure functions of the state, with no decisions in them. Stage 3 is an offline flow that runs once, and stages 5–8 are an online flow that runs every control sample. The two flows call the same foundation code, so there is no separate planning model that could drift from the control model, and they meet only at verification.

Only α crosses. Four outputs times six Bézier coefficients: 24 numbers. Not the solver and not the step duration T — the controller reads the robot's own leg angle rather than a clock, so a disturbance re-indexes the gait instead of desynchronizing it. T is an output of the design, never an input to the control.

The robot changed under this chapter

The 2026-09-02 model regeneration took RABBIT from 30 kg to 74 kg, non-uniformly: torso 10 → 47 kg, femurs 5 → 10, tibias 5 → 3.5. The published RABBIT is about 32 kg, so today's model is not the published robot, and any comparison with published numbers should be read with that in mind. The numbers in Chapter3/README.md and docs/CH3.md were measured before it, on a reference gait that is no longer an orbit of the robot; since 2026-09-17 both carry the numbers below. This report re-measures everything on today's model, on ch3_gait_posture_195: the gait Chapter 4 inherits, and verified here against today's dynamics.

§ 3.1  ·  stage 1

The hybrid model: one KKT matrix, used twice

In single support the stance foot is pinned, and holding it takes a contact force λ. The accelerations and that force come out of one saddle-point system:

[ M −J_stᵀ ] [ q̈ ] [ Bu − V − G ] [ J_st 0 ] [ λ ] = [ −J̇_st q̇ ] q̈ = q̈_drift + q̈_in · u λ = λ_drift + λ_in · u exact, by superposition

The left-hand matrix contains no u, so a single factorization with five right-hand sides — one drift, four input directions — recovers the affine dependence exactly. That exactness is what the rest of the chapter stands on: stage 4 builds an exact decoupling matrix from q̈_in, and stage 8 can write the friction cone and the normal-force floor as linear rows in u, because λ is affine in it. The test suite checks f + g·u against the raw KKT solve (1.7×10⁻¹³) and λ against it (1.3×10⁻¹³).

The impact is the same matrix with a different meaning: a momentum balance, not a force balance, solving for post-impact velocities and an impulse, with the swing-foot Jacobian enforcing J_sw q̇⁺ = 0 — the foot strikes and sticks (6.7×10⁻¹⁶). The legs are relabelled after the impact, never before: relabel first and J_sw describes the old stance foot, which already satisfies the constraint, so the impulse comes out exactly zero and the impact silently does nothing.

§ 3.2  ·  stages 2 and 4

Virtual constraints indexed by the robot, not the clock

The desired motion is indexed by the absolute stance-leg angle,

θ = q_T + q₁ + q₂/2 = c · q, s = (θ − θ⁻) / (θ⁺ − θ⁻), θ⁻ = −0.15, θ⁺ = 0.30 rad y = H q − y_d(s(q), α) 4 actuated joints, Bézier degree 5: α is 4 × 6

so s runs 0 → 1 across a step and the resulting feedback is time-invariant. Two details matter downstream. θ is linear in q, so ∂s/∂q is a constant row and L_f²y carries a single curvature term instead of the Hessian of the phase. And s is clamped only outside [−0.5, 1.5], not at [0, 1]: every step begins at s = 0, floating point puts it on either side, and a hard clamp there would intermittently zero ṡ and drop a term from ẏ.

The outputs have relative degree two, and substituting the exact split of §3.1 gives

ÿ = L_f²y + L_gL_f y · u, L_f²y = J_y q̈_drift − (∂²y_d/∂s²) ṡ², L_gL_f y = J_y q̈_in u = u_ff + (L_gL_f y)⁻¹ μ, u_ff = −(L_gL_f y)⁻¹ L_f²y ⇒ ÿ = μ

The suite checks ÿ against the derivative of the true flow (1.5×10⁻⁹), never reusing the formula, and that u_ff alone holds ÿ = 0 (1.1×10⁻¹⁴). This is partial feedback linearization, and partial by necessity: on the contact manifold single support is 10-dimensional, the transverse state η = [y; ẏ] is 8, and the remaining two dimensions are the zero dynamics in (θ, θ̇). Feedback owns η; only the choice of α shapes the other two.

§ 3.3  ·  stage 3

Collocation: what the solve can and cannot be trusted with

The gait is found by direct collocation. With h = T/(N − 1), each interval contributes a Hermite–Simpson defect, and driving the defects to zero is the dynamics constraint — there is no ODE solver inside the optimizer:

x_m = (x_k + x_k+1)/2 + (h/8)(f_k − f_k+1) ζ_k = x_k+1 − x_k − (h/6)(f_k + 4 f_m + f_k+1) J = (1 / L_step) ∫₀ᵀ ‖u‖² dt energy per distance: without the division, stepping in place is cheapest

The decision vector z = [X; T; α] has 14N + 25 entries: at the reference gait's N = 61, 879 unknowns against 869 equalities (node 1 on Z, the defects, node N on the guard, periodicity through Δ with the translation p_x left free). α is what the solve is for; the 854 node states exist only to make the dynamics algebraic. Inequalities sit in gated rows — 18 of them — and a gate that is off holds its row at −1 rather than removing it, so the problem's dimensions never change between warm starts.

The gate: small defects do not mean a real trajectory

Satisfied defects mean the discrete equations hold. On a mesh too coarse for the dynamics, the optimizer will happily return a spurious discrete solution, and every number derived from it is fiction. ch3_col_verify therefore integrates the closed loop from node 1 at RelTol 10⁻¹¹ and compares it with the node states; ch3_report prints REJECT above 10⁻³. The reference gait passes at 1.6×10⁻⁴, and a forward simulation under PD reproduces it: 0.427 m in 0.273 s on all six simulated steps.

The transcription is fifth order; the default basis is not smooth enough to show it

Hermite–Simpson's local defect should fall as h⁵. The suite's order check, run on the seed rollout under the default basis — a degree-3 clamped B-spline — fails on today's model at a median order of 0.58. § 3.4  ·  periodic is not stable

Two stability certificates that agree

The periodicity equality makes the start state a fixed point of the step-to-step map, and a fixed point can repel however small its residual. Two independent certificates are computed. The direct one central-differences the Poincaré map in the 13 directions other than p_x and returns the spectral radius ρ — 26 step simulations. The indirect one reduces the dynamics on Z to a scalar ODE a(θ)θ̈ + b(θ)θ̇² + c(θ) = 0 by projecting onto the covector that annihilates both the actuators and the contact wrench. An integrating factor then makes the step map affine in ζ = σ²/2, so its fixed point and eigenvalue are closed form, from α alone.

ρ and δ² agree to 5×10⁻⁵. δ² is the zero-dynamics eigenvalue, so the full map's ρ should not fall below it, and it does not. The suite checks the reduction against the full 14-state model (5.0×10⁻¹⁰) and that ½σ² + V_zero(θ) is conserved along a real rollout (2.3×10⁻⁶) — an invariant that cannot hold by accident if the integrating factor is wrong.

The certificate from α is available as a solve-time constraint (the hzd gate, on in this gait's solve). Because this transcription imposes periodicity directly, a converged solve already sits at a fixed point, so NEC4/NEC5 act as a check on the fixed point found rather than the mechanism that finds one. Both certificates are local: they describe the linearization about the orbit and do not bound its basin of attraction.

§ 3.5  ·  stages 5–8

Four ways to pick μ, and what the minimum-norm one costs

Every controller receives the same L_f²y, L_gL_f y and u_ff and differs only in μ:

  • PD on η, with K_p = 100, K_d = 20; its matching certificate is the Lyapunov equation AᵀP + PA = −Q.
  • RES-CLF: V = ηᵀP_εη with the required rate V̇ ≤ −(c₃/ε)V. From the CARE, c₃ = 0.366; from the Lyapunov equation, 0.381.
  • CLF-QP: the least-norm μ that meets that rate, in closed form (against quadprog, 9.0×10⁻¹¹). It rides the bound at a ratio of exactly 1.0000.
  • Constrained CLF-QP: the same QP with the torque box and the friction and normal-force rows — linear because λ is affine in u — and a slack δ on the CLF row, penalized by 10⁶, so that a conflict relaxes the rate instead of making the QP infeasible.

The constrained QP must run as sampled data. Its u(x) is only piecewise smooth — it kinks at every active-set switch — and an adaptive integrator reads each kink as a failed error test and stalls. Solving once per 1 ms and holding the torque makes the integrand smooth within a period. The suite checks that the hold's error is first order in the period (halving ratios 1.97 and 2.00).

Steps are capped at 1 s of robot time; the gait's own step is 0.273 s. The Chapter 3 simulator has no fall detection of its own — a guard crossing ends a step even when the robot is on the ground — which is why the fall criterion is applied here. It also pins the stance foot, modelling neither lift-off nor slip, so the valid column scores each run against the contact it assumes: the steps completed before the first sample with Fz ≤ 0 or |Fx|/Fz > 0.4. By that measure only PD walks the whole experiment; both CLF-QP forms slip within a step or two, so "falls in step 3" flatters the unconstrained law. Rerunning the table at ε = 0.20, the value Chapter 4 adopts, delays the falls by a step (3, 1, 2, 4) but leaves every valid count unchanged.

Minimum-norm is not the same as well-behaved

The unconstrained CLF-QP asks for exactly the convergence the RES-CLF certifies, and at the chapter's ε = 0.5 that is not much: c₃/ε = 0.732 is a time constant of 1.37 s against a 0.273 s step, so meeting the rate exactly contracts V by only 18% per step (e^−0.732·0.273). The impact then kicks η, and nothing in the continuous-phase guarantee speaks to Δ. PD is not tied to that rate — it transiently exceeds the CARE bound but converges further overall — and settles at max‖η‖ 0.21 on the same gait. That comparison runs four steps, and PD is not held to the torque box. The surplus convergence that minimum-norm optimization removes so efficiently is what was paying for stability across the impact.

The constrained QP does what stage 8 promises and no more. The torque never leaves the box, by construction, and δ reports the conflict visibly — the controller announcing that it cannot meet the rate inside the limit, where a saturating PD would void its guarantee silently. But its core is the same minimum-norm law, and on this 74 kg gait no box it was given kept it walking. This is where Chapter 4 starts: before it measures anything under model error, it tightens ε from 0.5 to 0.20, because an impact-marginal reference law would make every later comparison measure the reference rather than the robustness.

3. Step length is not a design variable in this transcription

If slow walking on a long stride is what exhausts the contact, the natural move is to shorten the stride: less leg extension at the end of stance, so Fz stays higher and less friction is needed. That route is closed here.

Testing it added row 19 to ch3_col_constraints (L_step ≤ p.limits.step_len_max, gated and off by default, so no earlier solve changes). The result was uniform: the stride will not come in by even one millimetre. In every run the violation stays exactly constant, the equalities hold, the stride moves about 10⁻⁵ m and the solver's step size then collapses to 10⁻⁸. What was tried:

So in this transcription the step length is an output of the dynamics, the periodicity condition and the virtual constraints — not something that can be traded. That is a statement about this formulation and this solver, not a proof that no short-stride gait exists: a different parametrisation — a shorter phase sweep, another posture branch, or a cold solve built around a short stride from the start — might well find one. What this rules out is reaching it by continuation from the gaits that exist.

4. The default basis costs transcription accuracy

The degree-3 B-spline default leaves the maximum defect three to four orders of magnitude above the Bézier basis at useful meshes, and the order check fails because of it.

What carries forward

What this hands to Chapter 4

Chapter 4 does not design a gait. It takes posture_195 and asks what happens when the robot is not the model: a 30% lighter, 50% heavier or three-times heavier robot, or one carrying up to 70 kg it is never told about. It inherits the whole foundation — the exact affine split becomes the true-versus-nominal split, and u_ff becomes the nominal pre-control whose mismatch defines Δ₁ and Δ₂. It inherits the sampled 1 kHz loop. And it inherits the one lesson of §3.5 as its first design decision: ε tightened from 0.5 to 0.20, so that the reference law re-converges between impacts before anything is perturbed.

Limitations to carry with it

  • One verified family of gaits. Everything verified on today's model descends from a single re-converged seed; a cold start has not produced a gait.
  • Local certificates. ρ and δ² describe the linearization about the orbit, not its basin, and the impact removes 30% of the kinetic energy every step.
  • Limits set by what was found, not by the hardware. The 195 Nm box is the lowest that verified, not an actuator rating.
