\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This document describes a modular MATLAB framework for modeling, simulation,
control, and trajectory optimization of the RABBIT planar five--link
underactuated biped. The centrepiece is a working Hybrid Zero Dynamics (HZD)
gait--optimization pipeline that produces dynamically feasible, periodic walking
gaits and animates them. We cover the robot model, the continuous and hybrid
dynamics, the HZD control formulation, the nonlinear program solved with
\code{fmincon} in both of its transcriptions --- single shooting and
Hermite--Simpson direct collocation --- the software architecture and data
flow, a per--folder file reference, and reproducibility notes. Deviations between the documented and the
as--built code (broken legacy entry points, orphaned files) are flagged
explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}
% =====================================================================
RABBIT is a planar, five--link, \emph{underactuated} biped: a torso plus two
legs, each with a hip and a knee. Walking is modelled as a hybrid dynamical
system --- continuous single--support (swing) dynamics punctuated by a discrete
impact when the swing foot strikes the ground. A gait is designed by imposing
\emph{virtual constraints} (B--spline reference trajectories for the actuated
joints) and choosing their coefficients, together with the periodic initial
state, so that the closed--loop system admits a stable periodic orbit while
minimizing actuator effort.

\paragraph{Dependencies.}
MATLAB with the \textbf{Symbolic Math Toolbox} (only to \emph{regenerate} the
dynamics; the generated files are committed) and the \textbf{Optimization
Toolbox} (\code{fmincon}, SQP --- required for the gait pipeline).

\paragraph{Working entry point.}
\begin{lstlisting}
startup                              % add all folders to the path
cd Trajectory_Optimization
rabbit_hzd_trajectory_optimization   % optimize one gait, inspect, animate
\end{lstlisting}
A gait library across walking speeds is built with \fname{build\_gait\_library.m}.
See Section~\ref{sec:known} for legacy entry points that no longer run.

\section{Robot model}
% =====================================================================
The generalized coordinates (7 DOF) are
\begin{equation}
  q = \begin{bmatrix} p_x & y & q_t & q_1 & q_2 & q_3 & q_4 \end{bmatrix}^{\!\top},
  \qquad
  x = \begin{bmatrix} q \\ \dot q \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{14}.
\end{equation}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}clc@{}}
\toprule
symbol & meaning & actuated \\
\midrule
$p_x, y$   & torso base position (world frame) & no \\
$q_t$      & torso pitch angle                 & no \\
$q_1, q_2$ & stance--leg hip, knee             & \textbf{yes} \\
$q_3, q_4$ & swing--leg hip, knee              & \textbf{yes} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Generalized coordinates. Four actuators drive $q_1\dots q_4$; the
floating base $(p_x,y,q_t)$ is unactuated, giving one degree of
underactuation in single support.}
\end{table}

The input map (\fname{Dynamics/input\_matrix.m}) is the constant
\begin{equation}
  B = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{3\times 4} \\ I_{4} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{7\times 4}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Physical parameters.}
There is no separate model/parameter file; the physical parameters are baked
into the symbolic derivation
(\fname{Dynamics/rabbit\_energy\_model\_generalized\_Lagrange.m}, nested
\code{Mass\_Properties}): torso $10\,$kg / $0.75\,$m; each leg link (thigh,
shank) $5\,$kg / $0.5\,$m; gravity $g = 9.8062\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.

\paragraph{Sign convention (important).}
The world--frame $Z$ axis is \emph{up--positive} (ground $=0$, above ground
$>0$), but $y$ is stored \emph{down--positive} in the generalized
coordinates, so the world--frame hip height is $-y$. All kinematic helpers
(\code{P\_st}, \code{P\_sw}, \code{Tt}, \code{get\_body\_points}) return
world--frame, up--positive heights. Mixing the two conventions has historically
caused sign bugs.

The name $y$ follows the symbolic derivation, where the second generalized
coordinate is declared as \code{y}
(\fname{rabbit\_energy\_model\_generalized\_Lagrange.m}, line 9), and the base
transform carries it as $-y$ in the world--vertical row. \warn{Note the symbol
clash:} $y$ also denotes the \emph{virtual--constraint output} of
Section~\ref{sec:hzd}. They are told apart by context and by arity --- the
output is always written with an argument, $y(q)$ or $y(x)$, and is a
$4$--vector; the coordinate is a scalar and appears only inside $q$.

\section{Continuous dynamics}
% =====================================================================
The unconstrained equations of motion are
\begin{equation}
  M(q)\,\ddot q + V(q,\dot q) + G(q) = B\,u,
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with $M(q)$ the mass/inertia matrix (\fname{Dynamics/M.m}), $V(q,\dot q)$ the
Coriolis/centrifugal vector (\fname{Dynamics/V.m}, called as
\code{V([q;dq])}), and $G(q)$ the gravity vector (\fname{Dynamics/G.m}).

\paragraph{Single--support (constrained) dynamics.}
During swing the stance foot is pinned by the holonomic contact constraint
$p_{\mathrm{st}}(q)=\text{const}$, i.e. at the acceleration level
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{st}}(q)\,\ddot q + \dot J_{\mathrm{st}}\dot q = 0,
  \qquad J_{\mathrm{st}} = \frac{\partial p_{\mathrm{st}}}{\partial q}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 7}.
\end{equation}
\fname{Dynamics/rabbit\_constrained\_dynamics.m} solves the resulting
saddle--point (KKT) system for the accelerations and the contact wrench
$\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} M & -J_{\mathrm{st}}^{\top} \\ J_{\mathrm{st}} & 0 \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ddot q \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} B u - V - G \\ -\dot J_{\mathrm{st}}\dot q \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

% =====================================================================
\section{Hybrid model}
% =====================================================================
Position is continuous across impact; only velocity jumps.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Guard / event} (\fname{Contact/rabbit\_impact\_event.m}): the
        swing--foot height $p_{\mathrm{sw},z}(q)$ crosses zero from above.
  \item \textbf{Impact map} (\fname{Contact/rabbit\_impact\_map.m}):
        momentum--conserving reset enforcing zero post--impact swing--foot
        velocity,
        \begin{equation}
          \begin{bmatrix} M & -J_{\mathrm{sw}}^{\top} \\ J_{\mathrm{sw}} & 0 \end{bmatrix}
          \begin{bmatrix} \dot q^{+} \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
          =
          \begin{bmatrix} M\,\dot q^{-} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}.
        \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Reset map} (\fname{Reset\_Map/rabbit\_reset\_map.m}): relabels
        stance/swing legs (\fname{relabel\_state.m}) and re--plants the new
        stance foot on the ground ($y \mathrel{+}= p_{\mathrm{st},z}$). The
        horizontal coordinate is intentionally not shifted, so the robot walks
        forward continuously in the world frame.
\end{itemize}

\section{Phase variable and Hybrid Zero Dynamics}
\label{sec:hzd}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{Phase variable (linear in $q$).}
The gait ``clock'' is the absolute stance--leg angle, \emph{linear} in $q$:
\begin{equation}
  \theta(q) = q_t + q_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}q_2 = c\,q,
  \qquad c = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & \tfrac12 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:theta}
\end{equation}
This is implemented in \fname{Controller/theta\_of\_q.m}; its gradient
$\partial\theta/\partial q = c$ is the constant returned by
\fname{Controller/dtheta\_dq\_of.m}. Because both leg links have length
$\tfrac12$, the geometric hip--to--foot angle $\operatorname{atan2}$ equals
\eqref{eq:theta} exactly for all physical poses ($|q_2|<\pi$); the linear form
is preferred because \emph{it cannot wrap at $\pm\pi$} and matches Westervelt's
Hypothesis HH6, on which the HZD decoupling analysis relies.

\paragraph{Virtual constraints.}
The normalized phase is
\begin{equation}
  s = \frac{\theta - \theta^{-}}{\theta^{+} - \theta^{-}} \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
where $\theta^{-}$ is the phase at step start and $\theta^{+}$
(\code{p.theta\_plus}) at touchdown. The four actuated joints track a clamped
cubic B--spline reference $y_d(s)$,
\begin{equation}
  y(q) = q_{4:7} - y_d(s), \qquad u = -K_p\,y - K_d\,\dot y,
\end{equation}
with $K_p=400$, $K_d=40$. The B--spline is evaluated by
\fname{bspline\_eval.m} (basis functions from \fname{BSpline.m} /
\fname{BSpline\_derivative.m}); the whole closed loop is assembled in
\fname{Dynamics/hzd\_closed\_loop\_ode.m}, whose augmented state
$\xi = [x;\,\text{torque cost}]$ integrates the running cost alongside the
dynamics.

\section{The Chapter~3 package: three core functions}
\label{sec:core}
% =====================================================================
\fname{Chapter3/} is a separate, from--scratch implementation of the Chapter~3
pipeline. It reuses only the symbolically generated model in \fname{Dynamics/};
everything above that layer is new. Three of its functions contain almost all of
the non--obvious reasoning in the package, and are documented here in full. A
per--function inventory and call graph is in \fname{docs/CH3\_FUNCTION\_MAP.md}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\code{ch3\_control\_affine}: building $f$ and $g$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
Chapter~3 requires the continuous phase in control--affine form,
$\dot x = f(x) + g(x)u$. This is stronger than being able to evaluate $\ddot q$
for a given $u$: three later stages need the $u$--dependence separated
\emph{symbolically} --- the decoupling matrix $L_gL_f y = J_y\,\ddot q_{\text{in}}$
of Section~\ref{sec:core}, the requirement that $\ddot y$ be affine in $u$ so the
stage--7 problem is a QP at all, and the requirement that the ground reaction
force be affine in $u$ so the friction cone is a \emph{linear} inequality.

Starting from \eqref{eq:eom} with the stance foot pinned, the contact wrench
enters as the generalized force $J_{\mathrm{st}}^{\top}\lambda$, giving the
saddle--point system
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} M & -J^{\top} \\ J & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{A}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ddot q \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} Bu - V - G \\ -\dot J\dot q \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad J \equiv J_{\mathrm{st}}.
  \label{eq:kkt}
\end{equation}
The contact force is therefore not modelled but \emph{solved for}, as the
Lagrange multiplier of the same system that yields the accelerations.

\paragraph{The five right--hand sides.}
$A$ contains no $u$, and $u$ enters the right--hand side linearly. One
factorization with five right--hand sides --- one drift column and four input
directions --- therefore recovers the whole affine structure by superposition:
\begin{lstlisting}
rhs = [ [-V_vec - G_vec], B_mat ; ...
        [-Jdotdq],        zeros(nc, nu) ];
sol = A \ rhs;             % (nq+nc) x (1+nu)
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{equation}
  \ddot q = \ddot q_{\text{drift}} + \ddot q_{\text{in}}\,u,
  \qquad
  \lambda = \lambda_{\text{drift}} + \lambda_{\text{in}}\,u,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  f(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \dot q \\ \ddot q_{\text{drift}}\end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  g(x) = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{7\times4} \\ \ddot q_{\text{in}}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
This is exact --- not linearized, not finite--differenced; it is the solution of
\eqref{eq:kkt}, rearranged. The structural zero in the top block of $g$ (torque
cannot change position instantaneously) is precisely what makes the outputs
relative degree~2.

Finite--differencing $g$ instead would cost five solves \emph{plus} truncation
error, and every downstream quantity inherits it: $L_gL_fy \to u_{\mathrm{ff}}
\to$ the collocation cost $\to$ every gradient \code{fmincon} estimates.
Measured, the batched solve is also $3.5\times$ faster
($9.6\,\mu$s versus $33.2\,\mu$s).

\paragraph{Numerical character.}
Over all nodes of the reference gait, $\operatorname{cond}(A)\in[457,491]$; over
4000 random poses the median is $428$ and the maximum $1.07\times10^{3}$, with
$J_{\mathrm{st}}$ never losing rank. Two structural notes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $A$ is \emph{not} symmetric ($\lVert A-A^{\top}\rVert_\infty = 5.88$),
        because the upper--right block is $-J^{\top}$ while the lower--left is
        $+J$. Negating one block row would give the symmetric indefinite form
        admitting $LDL^{\top}$. The present arrangement is equally valid and
        returns $\lambda$ directly in the desired convention --- the force the
        \emph{ground applies to the robot}, $F_z>0$ pushing up (measured
        $F_z = +39.75\,$N at $u=0$). At $9\times9$ the solver choice is
        immaterial.
  \item \warn{The solve is unguarded.} \code{ch3\_io\_lin} checks \code{rcond}
        and falls back to \code{pinv}; this function checks nothing. The
        justification holds --- $M$ is positive definite always and
        $J_{\mathrm{st}}$ for a planar point foot is generically full row rank,
        with zero rank failures in 4000 random poses --- but a singular $A$ would
        propagate \code{NaN} into an ODE step rather than raise a diagnosis.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two details worth preserving.}
The local names \code{M\_mat}, \code{V\_vec}, \code{G\_vec} avoid shadowing the
functions \code{M}, \code{V}, \code{G} on the path --- writing \code{M = M(q)}
would make the next call in scope index the matrix instead. And
\code{nc = size(J,1)} reads the contact count from the Jacobian rather than
hardcoding it, so a different contact model propagates automatically. The
\code{aux} struct is assembled only when \code{nargout > 2}, which matters when
\code{ode45} calls the function tens of thousands of times per step.

\subsection{\code{ch3\_io\_lin}: from a nonlinear robot to $\ddot y = \mu$}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
The outputs have relative degree~2: $u$ is a torque, so it cannot appear until
one has differentiated down to $\ddot q$. Because $\partial s/\partial q$ is a
\emph{constant} row (Hypothesis HH6, \eqref{eq:theta}), the derivative of the
output Jacobian collapses to a single term,
\begin{equation}
  \dot J_y = -\frac{d^2y_d}{ds^2}\,\dot s\,\frac{\partial s}{\partial q},
  \qquad
  \dot J_y\dot q = -\frac{d^2y_d}{ds^2}\,\dot s^{\,2},
\end{equation}
so that, substituting $\ddot q = \ddot q_{\text{drift}} + \ddot q_{\text{in}}u$,
\begin{equation}
  \ddot y = \underbrace{J_y\ddot q_{\text{drift}} - \tfrac{d^2y_d}{ds^2}\dot s^{\,2}}_{L_f^2y\ \ (4\times1)}
          + \underbrace{J_y\ddot q_{\text{in}}}_{L_gL_fy\ \ (4\times4)} u .
\end{equation}
Were $\theta$ nonlinear in $q$, $\dot J_y$ would additionally carry the
\emph{Hessian of the phase} --- a $7\times7$ second--derivative tensor to build
and differentiate at every timestep. That is the concrete value of the linear
phase variable. Both terms are exact: $\ddot q_{\text{in}}$ from \eqref{eq:kkt},
$d^2y_d/ds^2$ analytically from the B\'ezier basis.

Inverting the decoupling matrix gives
\begin{equation}
  u = u_{\mathrm{ff}} + (L_gL_fy)^{-1}\mu,
  \qquad
  u_{\mathrm{ff}} = -(L_gL_fy)^{-1}L_f^2y
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \ddot y = \mu .
\end{equation}
Verified independently by central--differencing $\dot y(x)$ along the true flow
$\dot x = f+gu$, never reusing the $\ddot y$ formula: $u=u_{\mathrm{ff}}$ gives
$\max|\ddot y| = 2.55\times10^{-9}$; a requested
$\mu = [1.5,\,-2,\,0.7,\,3]^{\top}$ is delivered to $2.48\times10^{-9}$; and
$\mu = e_k$ moves only channel $k$, reproducing the identity to $10^{-9}$. Peak
$|u_{\mathrm{ff}}|$ over the reference step is $106.6\,$Nm. Every controller in
stages 5--8 differs \emph{only} in how it selects $\mu$.

\paragraph{What is not linearized.}
On the constraint manifold single support is $10$--dimensional while
$\eta = [y;\dot y]$ is $8$, leaving a $2$--dimensional zero dynamics
$(\theta,\dot\theta)$. This is \emph{partial} feedback linearization, and
partial by necessity: full--state feedback linearization requires full
actuation, and there are four actuators for five effective DOF. Feedback handles
the transverse directions; the choice of $\alpha$ handles the pendulum.

\paragraph{Conditioning, and a correction to the source comment.}
Measured: over the gait $\operatorname{rcond}(L_gL_fy)\in[5.61,8.34]\times10^{-3}$
($\operatorname{cond}\in[80,128]$); over 20\,000 random states spanning the full
$\pm\pi$ joint box the worst value is $8.05\times10^{-6}$. \textbf{The
\code{pinv} fallback never fires} --- zero times in 20\,000 states. It is
genuinely defensive.

The source comment attributes near--singularity to \emph{``knee lock, or if
$dy_d/ds$ drives $J_y$ toward a rank--deficient combination.''} Measured, the two
halves do not fare equally:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \warn{Knee lock: not supported.} Driving the stance knee to full
        extension leaves conditioning flat
        ($q_2 = 1.347 \to \operatorname{rcond} 5.76\times10^{-3}$;
         $q_2 = 0 \to 7.03\times10^{-3}$). A $121\times121$ grid over
        \emph{both} knees across $(-\pi,\pi)$ puts the worst case at
        $\operatorname{rcond} = 2.14\times10^{-7}$ at
        $(q_2,q_4)=(2.125,1.866)$ --- deep double--knee \emph{flexion}
        ($\approx122^\circ$ and $\approx107^\circ$), not extension, and still
        five orders above the $10^{-12}$ threshold.
  \item \textbf{Profile slope: supported.} Stretching $\alpha$ about $\alpha_0$
        degrades conditioning monotonically: a $100\times$ steeper profile costs
        about $1.75$ orders of magnitude of \code{rcond}
        ($5.76\times10^{-3} \to 1.01\times10^{-4}$).
\end{itemize}

The docstring has since been corrected accordingly: it now states the
profile--slope mechanism, records that knee angle barely matters, and notes that
the \code{pinv} fallback has never been observed to fire.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{\code{ch3\_impact}: the same matrix, a different meaning}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
The impact solves a system structurally identical to \eqref{eq:kkt} --- same
saddle--point shape, same $M$, a foot Jacobian and a multiplier --- while
everything it \emph{means} differs.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & \code{ch3\_control\_affine} & \code{ch3\_impact} \\
\midrule
unknowns   & accelerations $\ddot q$      & post--impact velocities $\dot q^{+}$ \\
multiplier & contact \emph{force} [N]     & contact \emph{impulse} [Ns] \\
Jacobian   & $J_{\mathrm{st}}$, pinned foot & $J_{\mathrm{sw}}$, foot about to land \\
right side & forces $Bu-V-G$              & momentum $M\dot q^{-}$ \\
balance    & force                        & momentum \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The two KKT systems of the hybrid model. Configuration $q$ does not
appear on the right of the impact system because it is \emph{continuous} through
impact; $M$ and $J_{\mathrm{sw}}$ are both evaluated at the pre--impact
configuration. The bottom row imposes $J_{\mathrm{sw}}\dot q^{+}=0$: the foot
strikes and sticks, with no rebound and no slip.}
\end{table}

Measured: $\operatorname{cond} = 490.8$, essentially that of the continuous
system, and $\lVert J_{\mathrm{sw}}\dot q^{+}\rVert = 4.8\times10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{The impact is violently dissipative.}
On the reference gait the kinetic energy falls
$70.06 \to 20.27\,$J --- \textbf{71.1\,\% destroyed in an instant} --- with an
impulse of $[-11.85,\,4.86]^{\top}$, norm $12.81\,$Ns (the Table~3.1 impulse
quantity). This is the physical content of ``plastic'': the ground absorbs
whatever is required to stop the foot dead. It is also why the impact dominates
the hybrid stability budget, and why $\varepsilon$ in the stage--5 law is
squeezed --- the controller has exactly one step to undo what $\Delta$ does in
zero time.

\paragraph{Order is not negotiable.}
$\Delta$ is impact, then relabel, then re--plant. Swapping the first two does not
merely perturb the result:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
wrong--order impulse & $[0.000,\ 0.000]$ versus correct $[-11.851,\ 4.858]$ \\
velocity error & $\lVert\dot q^{+}_{\text{correct}}-\dot q^{+}_{\text{wrong}}\rVert = 13.85$ (correct norm $5.49$) \\
true swing foot & left sliding at $0.663$ instead of $4.8\times10^{-16}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\warn{The impulse is exactly zero --- the impact becomes a no--op.} The reason is
worth understanding rather than memorizing: \code{ch3\_relabel} swaps the leg
coordinate slots, so after relabelling, $J_{\mathrm{sw}}$ evaluated on the new
coordinates describes the \emph{old stance} foot. That foot was pinned and
already satisfies $J\dot q = 0$; the constraint the solver is asked to enforce is
already met, the multiplier is zero, and nothing happens. The code runs and
returns plausible numbers.

\paragraph{Re--plant, and the coordinate left alone.}
Since $y$ is down--positive, $\text{foot}_z = -y - (\text{joint terms})$, so
adding $\text{foot}_z$ to $y$ drives the new stance foot to exactly $z=0$. On a
collocation solution the correction is negligible ($1.7\times10^{-12}\,$m ---
node $N$ is constrained to the guard), but in forward simulation the event
tolerance leaves a real residual, and without the re--plant the contact point
ratchets downward across steps. $p_x$ is deliberately \emph{not} reset (measured
$\Delta p_x = 0$ through $\Delta$): it is the translation gauge and must
accumulate, which is why periodicity is imposed on $13$ components and not $14$.

\end{table}

\paragraph{The HH6 identity, stated precisely.}
The geometric identity is
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{atan2}\!\big(-v_x,\,-v_z\big) = q_t + q_1 + \tfrac12 q_2
  \pmod{2\pi},
  \qquad v = p_{\mathrm{st}} - p_{\mathrm{hip}},
\end{equation}
verified to $5.3\times10^{-15}$ over 5000 random poses with either knee anywhere
in $(-\pi,\pi)$. What is exact for all poses is the \emph{direction}; the
hip--to--foot \emph{distance} is $\cos(q_2/2)$ (verified to $4.4\times10^{-16}$),
so the leg shortens as the knee bends and equals the straight--leg length $1$
only at $q_2=0$. Writing $L=\tfrac12$ for the link length,
$\sin a_1 + \sin(a_1{+}q_2) = 2\sin(a_1{+}\tfrac{q_2}{2})\cos\tfrac{q_2}{2}$ and
likewise for the cosine, so $\cos(q_2/2)$ factors out as magnitude and the angle
$a_1 + q_2/2$ is what remains.

\subsection{Smoothness of \code{ch3\_yd}}
\label{sec:ydsmooth}
Because $d^2y_d/ds^2$ enters $L_f^2y$ directly, the output profile must be $C^2$
and the derivatives it returns must genuinely be its derivatives. Both were
tested. The default B\'ezier basis passes unchanged; the optional B--spline
basis had two defects, \textbf{both since fixed} --- the before/after contrast
is drawn in Fig.~\ref{fig:ydsmooth}(c,d) and described below, because it is the
reason the code is written the way it is.

\paragraph{B\'ezier: smooth, and the derivatives are correct.}
Against central finite differences on $s\in[-0.4,1.4]$ with $h=10^{-5}$,
$\max|dy_d - \tfrac{d}{ds}y_d| = 3.74\times10^{-9}$ and
$\max|d^2y_d - \tfrac{d}{ds}dy_d| = 7.22\times10^{-9}$. (Comparing $d^2y_d$
against the \emph{second}--difference of $y_d$ gives $1.05\times10^{-4}$, which is
that formula's own roundoff, $\approx\epsilon/h^2$, not an error in
\code{ch3\_yd}.) Scanning 200\,000 samples, every sample--to--sample step in
$y_d$, $dy_d$ and $d^2y_d$ sits just under the bound implied by the next
derivative --- $1.624\times10^{-4}$ against a Lipschitz bound of
$1.640\times10^{-4}$, and so on --- so there are no jumps. This is expected: a
degree--5 polynomial is $C^\infty$. It is now checked rather than assumed.

\paragraph{B--spline: the same curve, two defects since corrected.}
At matching degree (\code{bsp\_deg} $=5$ with $6$ control points) the B--spline
reproduces the B\'ezier curve to $6.66\times10^{-16}$ in value and
$1.78\times10^{-15}$ in slope, which confirms the degree--5
\code{BSpline\_derivative} fix. Two further problems were found and repaired:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The clamp made $dy_d$ inconsistent with $y_d$.} A clamped knot
        vector defines the curve only on $[0,1]$, so unlike the B\'ezier branch
        this one must clamp $s$. It did --- but then evaluated the slope at the
        clamped $s$ as well, so outside the interval $y_d$ froze constant while
        the reported $dy_d$ froze at the \emph{boundary slope} ($0.561$ below
        $s=0$, $0.883$ above $s=1$). A constant function has zero derivative, so
        the returned $dy_d$ was not the derivative of the returned $y_d$, and
        \code{ch3\_outputs} then built $J_y = H - dy_d\,\partial s/\partial q$
        from it and added a term that should not have been there. This also
        defeated the phase margin of Section~\ref{sec:hzd}: \code{ch3\_phase}
        deliberately allows $s\in[-0.5,1.5]$ because a polynomial is well
        behaved just outside $[0,1]$, but the B--spline branch reinstated a hard
        clamp at exactly $[0,1]$ --- a kink at $s=0$, the start of every step.
        \emph{Fix:} $dy_d$ and $d^2y_d$ are now zero whenever the clamp engages,
        matching what \code{ch3\_phase} does with $\partial s/\partial q$.
  \item \textbf{The second derivative degraded at touchdown.} It is obtained by
        differencing the analytic first derivative with $h=10^{-5}$, and the
        stencil was \emph{clipped} to $[0,1]$ --- which silently turns the
        central difference into a half--width one--sided formula, dropping it
        from $O(h^2)$ to $O(h)$. The error was flat at $\approx4\times10^{-9}$
        across the interior and jumped to $1.76\times10^{-4}$ once $1-s<h$. The
        affected neighbourhood is narrow, but $s=1$ is exactly where node $N$
        sits and where $|d^2y_d/ds^2|$ is largest on this gait ($5.85$ versus
        $\approx1$ mid--step), so it fed $L_f^2y$ at the worst moment.
        \emph{Fix:} the stencil is now kept entirely inside $[0,1]$, using a
        second--order one--sided formula within $h$ of an end. The error at
        $s=1$ falls from $1.76\times10^{-4}$ to $3.77\times10^{-9}$, matching
        interior accuracy.
\end{itemize}

Neither defect was reachable in the default configuration
(\code{p.basis = 'bezier'}), where no clamp exists and both derivatives are
closed--form; they affected only the B--spline cross--check path. All five
\code{ch3\_test\_*} suites pass after the change.

\section{Trajectory optimization (the nonlinear program)}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{Decision vector.}
\begin{equation}
  z = \begin{bmatrix} \operatorname{vec}(\text{coeffs}) \\ x_{\text{start}} \end{bmatrix}
    \in \mathbb{R}^{\,n_u n_c + 14} = \mathbb{R}^{38},
\end{equation}
with $n_u=4$ actuated outputs and $n_c=6$ control points each
(\fname{unpack\_z.m}).

\paragraph{Cost} (\fname{hzd\_cost.m}): squared torque per unit step length
(Westervelt eq.~6.43),
\begin{equation}
  J(z) = \frac{1}{L_{\text{step}}}\int_0^{T_{\text{step}}} \lVert u(t)\rVert_2^2\, dt,
\end{equation}
where $L_{\text{step}}$ is the stance--to--swing foot distance at impact.
Normalizing by $L_{\text{step}}$ removes the degenerate incentive to
``step in place''.

\paragraph{Constraints} (\fname{hzd\_constraints.m}). Equalities
($\mathrm{ceq}\in\mathbb{R}^{18}$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item one--step periodicity, $x_{\text{next}}(2\!:\!14) = x_{\text{start}}(2\!:\!14)$, with
        $x_{\text{next}} = \Delta_{\text{reset}}\!\big(\Delta_{\text{impact}}(x_{\text{end}})\big)$ \hfill (13);
  \item stance foot on ground at $t=0$ \hfill (1);
  \item stance foot zero velocity, $J_{\mathrm{st}}\dot q_{\text{start}}=0$ \hfill (2);
  \item touchdown phase, $\theta(q_{\text{end}}) = \theta^{+}$ \hfill (1);
  \item \textbf{NEC1} average walking rate, $L_{\text{step}}/T_{\text{step}} = v_{\text{des}}$ \hfill (1).
\end{itemize}
Inequalities ($c\in\mathbb{R}^{8}$, $c\le 0$): no ground penetration; mid--step
swing--foot clearance (NIC3); step--length floor; orbital stability
(Section~\ref{sec:stability}); and the four \emph{physical realizability}
constraints of Section~\ref{sec:physical} (torque, friction, ground reaction,
impact impulse). The stability and physical constraints are each individually
\emph{gated} (default off) --- see those sections. Disabled inequalities are
held at a trivially satisfied value so $c$ keeps constant length.

\paragraph{Solver} (\fname{hzd\_problem\_setup.m}). \code{fmincon} SQP with
\emph{central} finite differences (the event--triggered \code{ode45} makes
forward differences noisy) and \code{ScaleProblem} \emph{off} (so reported
feasibility is directly comparable to the constraint tolerance). Cold starts
from $x_0$ are unreliable; \fname{build\_gait\_library.m} warm--starts each
speed from the previously converged gait and marches $v_{\text{des}}$ in small
steps.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} \fname{inspect\_solution.m} prints and returns a full
breakdown of any $z$: the $\theta$ sweep, step length/duration, walking speed,
cost, every constraint residual, the worst violation, the within--step
stance--foot drift, and the orbital--stability radius $\rho$ (below).

\section{Direct collocation}
\label{sec:collocation}
% =====================================================================
Everything above transcribes the gait problem by \emph{single shooting}: the
decision variables are the B\'ezier coefficients and one initial state, and the
dynamics are satisfied implicitly because \code{ode45} integrates them.
\fname{Trajectory\_Optimization/Collocation/} implements the alternative
transcription --- \emph{Hermite--Simpson direct collocation} --- which matches
the thesis method. There is no \code{ode45} in the optimization loop at all.

\paragraph{What changes.} The trajectory itself becomes a decision variable.
For $N$ nodes the vector $z$ concatenates
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
block & size & meaning \\
\midrule
$X$      & $2n_q \times N$      & node states $[q;\dot q]$ \\
$U$      & $n_u \times N$       & node joint torques \\
$\Lambda$& $2 \times N$         & node stance--foot contact forces $[F_x;F_z]$ \\
\code{coeffs} & $n_u \times n_c$ & B\'ezier virtual--constraint parameters \\
$T$      & $1$                  & step duration \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(\fname{col\_pack.m} / \fname{col\_unpack.m}). With the defaults $n_q=7$,
$n_u=4$, $n_c=6$, $N=20$ that is $280+80+40+24+1 = 425$ variables, versus the
$38$ of the shooting formulation. The dynamics are then imposed as
\emph{equality constraints} --- the Hermite--Simpson defects between
consecutive nodes --- rather than being integrated. The virtual constraints
$y = q_{4:7} - y_d(s,\code{coeffs}) = 0$ are enforced at \emph{every} node, so
the solution lives on the hybrid zero--dynamics manifold by construction and
\code{coeffs} still parametrizes it.

\paragraph{The trade.} Collocation buys a much better conditioned problem: no
event--triggered integrator inside the finite differences, so the Jacobian is
sparse, exact in structure, and free of the integration noise that forced
central differences in the shooting setup. It pays for this with an order of
magnitude more variables and with a solution that is only \emph{as feasible as
its defect tolerance} --- which motivates the cross--check below.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_gait_stickfigure.png}
\caption{One converged collocation step
(\fname{col\_result\_2026-07-23\_00-45-04.mat}, $N=20$, $T=1.046$~s), sampled at
seven nodes and offset horizontally for legibility. Blue is the stance leg, red
the swing leg, green the torso; opacity increases through the step. The stance
foot stays planted while the swing leg advances and re--strikes ahead of it.}
\label{fig:stick}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Warm starting (\fname{rabbit\_hzd\_collocation.m}).} The driver
accepts either kind of saved gait and does the right thing with each:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a \emph{collocation} result (\fname{col\_result\_*.mat}, identified by
        carrying an \code{N} field) has its $z$ reused directly --- $N$ must
        match;
  \item a \emph{shooting/PD} result (\fname{hzd\_result\_*.mat}) is simulated
        for one step and resampled onto the $N$ nodes by
        \fname{col\_seed\_from\_pd.m}.
\end{itemize}
This is what makes the ``one constraint at a time'' workflow practical: enable
one physical constraint, re--solve warm--started from the last converged
collocation gait, repeat. \fname{col\_verify\_seed.m} reports the norm of every
equality block on a seed \emph{without solving}, which separates a genuine
transcription bug from a merely hard NLP.

\paragraph{Cross--checking the result (\fname{col\_crosscheck.m}).} A
collocation solution is an \textbf{open--loop} trajectory. It satisfies the
defect constraints, but nothing in the transcription guarantees that the
shooting simulator, driven by an actual feedback law, reproduces it. The
cross--check extracts \code{coeffs} and the node--1 state, simulates one step
under \code{p.controller} (\code{'pd'} or \code{'clfqp'}), and overlays the two
(Figure~\ref{fig:crosscheck}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_col_vs_closedloop.png}
\caption{Collocation reference (black) versus the PD closed--loop simulation

$0.026$~rad rms, $0.140$~rad max, the largest errors at the stance knee just

\paragraph{Stability of a collocation gait (\fname{col\_poincare.m}).} Same
Poincar\'e machinery as Section~\ref{sec:stability}, fed from a
\fname{col\_result\_*.mat}. Use \code{'pd'}: the CLF--QP law solves a QP per
ODE step, and $\rho$ costs $\approx 26$ step simulations.

\section{Orbital stability: periodic is not the same as stable}
\label{sec:stability}
% =====================================================================
This section is deliberately expanded for teaching, because it is the single
most instructive point in the pipeline.

\paragraph{The trap.} The periodicity equality makes $x_{\text{start}}$ a
\emph{fixed point} of the step--to--step (Poincar\'e) map
\begin{equation}
  x_{\text{next}} = P(x_{\text{start}})
      := \Delta_{\text{reset}}\big(\Delta_{\text{impact}}(\varphi_{\text{step}}(x_{\text{start}}))\big),
  \label{eq:poincare}
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_{\text{step}}$ is one closed--loop swing phase. But \emph{a fixed
point can be unstable.} Satisfying periodicity to machine precision guarantees a
limit cycle \emph{exists}; it says nothing about whether the robot stays on it.

\paragraph{The diagnosis.} Linearize \eqref{eq:poincare} about the fixed point,
$A = \partial P/\partial x$, and look at its spectral radius
\begin{equation}
  \rho = \max_i |\lambda_i(A)|.
\end{equation}
The gait is orbitally (asymptotically) stable iff $\rho < 1$. This is
Westervelt's stability requirement (informally, NEC5). For the reference seed
gait obtained by enforcing periodicity \emph{alone}, the numbers are stark:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
$\max|\text{periodicity}|$ & $\rho$ & open--loop $|\dot q|$ over 6 steps \\
\midrule
$0.007$ (excellent) & $\approx 3$ (unstable) &
  $6 \to 6 \to 19 \to 891 \to 949 \to 440$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The orbit is a near--perfect fixed point that the robot falls off after two
steps. Every downstream symptom we observed --- stance--foot drift growing
across a step, Baumgarte constraint stabilization diverging, the Poincar\'e
settling iteration blowing up, fragile speed continuation --- is the same
instability seen from a different angle: an orbit with $\rho>1$ amplifies
\emph{every} perturbation, numerical or physical.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_limit_cycle.png}
\caption{What $\rho>1$ looks like. Phase portraits of the four actuated joints
over consecutive PD closed--loop steps, started from the collocation gait's
node--1 state. Circles mark where each step \emph{begins}: on an orbitally
stable limit cycle they would coincide, and instead step~2 starts visibly away
from step~1 --- most starkly at the swing hip, which restarts with
$\approx 12.7$~rad/s where step~1 began near zero. Only two steps are drawn
because the third leaves the physical range entirely ($|q|$ reaching several
hundred radians), the same blow--up tabulated above. Compare
Figure~\ref{fig:crosscheck}: a \emph{single} step tracks its reference well,
which is exactly why one--step agreement is not evidence of a walkable gait.}
\label{fig:limitcycle}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The remedy.} Add $\rho < 1$ as an optimization constraint.
\fname{poincare\_stability.m} builds $A$ by central finite differences on the
13 periodic states (index $2\!:\!14$; $p_x$ is the translation gauge and is
excluded),
\begin{equation}
  A_{:,j} = \frac{P\!\left(x_{\text{start}} + h\,e_j\right) - P\!\left(x_{\text{start}} - h\,e_j\right)}{2h},
  \qquad j = 1,\dots,13,
\end{equation}
and returns $\rho = \max_i|\lambda_i(A)|$. \fname{hzd\_constraints.m} then adds
the inequality $\rho - \rho_{\max} \le 0$ (with $\rho_{\max}=0.95$).

\paragraph{Cost and workflow.} Each $\rho$ evaluation costs $\approx 2\times 13 =
26$ step simulations, and \code{fmincon} finite--differences the constraint over
$38$ variables --- hundreds to thousands of simulations per iteration. The
constraint is therefore \textbf{gated} behind \code{p.enforce\_stability}
(default \code{false}). The recommended workflow is two--phase:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item solve a \emph{periodic} gait with stability off (fast);
  \item turn \code{p.enforce\_stability = true} and re--solve
        \emph{warm--started} from that gait to drive $\rho$ below~1.
\end{enumerate}
\fname{inspect\_solution.m} reports $\rho$ on every call regardless of the gate,
so stability is always visible even when it is not being enforced.

\paragraph{Analogy.} Just as the \emph{step--length floor} was the missing
constraint that stopped the optimizer exploiting a degenerate ``step in place'',
the \emph{stability bound} is the missing constraint that stops it returning a
mathematically periodic but physically un--walkable orbit. In both cases the
optimizer was doing exactly what it was told; the specification was incomplete.

% =====================================================================
\section{Physical realizability (thesis Table 3.1)}
\label{sec:physical}
% =====================================================================
\paragraph{Two families of constraints.} It helps to see the whole set as doing
two different jobs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Mathematical validity} --- is this a \emph{real, attracting}
        gait? Periodicity (a fixed point), $\theta_{\text{end}}=\theta^{+}$
        (the phase closes), NEC1 (walking rate), and orbital stability
        ($\rho<1$, Section~\ref{sec:stability}). These say nothing about
        physics: a gait can satisfy all of them and be impossible to build.
  \item \textbf{Physical realizability} --- can the hardware and the ground
        actually produce it? The Table~3.1 constraints below.
\end{itemize}
Both are required, and ``periodic'' (cheap to satisfy) is a tiny subset of
``walkable'' (both families). All of the physical quantities are recovered from
the \emph{same} simulation the optimizer already runs: the contact force
$\lambda=[F_x;F_z]$ is an output of \fname{rabbit\_constrained\_dynamics}, the
joint torque $u$ of \fname{hzd\_control\_and\_dynamics}, and the impact impulse
is the multiplier inside \fname{rabbit\_impact\_map}. \fname{gait\_forces.m}
replays the trajectory and returns the peaks; \fname{hzd\_constraints.m} turns
them into inequalities.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{5.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
constraint & form (value) & what failure it prevents \\
\midrule
Vertical GRF   & $\min F_z \ge F_{z,\min}$ ($\ge 20$ N) &
  ground can only \emph{push} (unilateral contact); $F_z\!\le\!0$ means the foot lifts / the model invents a pulling force \\
Friction cone  & $\max|F_x/F_z| \le \mu$ ($0.4$) &
  stance foot \emph{slipping} (well posed only where $F_z>0$) \\
Joint torque   & $\max|u| \le u_{\max}$ ($120$ Nm) &
  commanding torque the actuator cannot deliver \\
Impact impulse & $|F_e| \le F_{e,\max}$ ($30$ Ns) &
  a violent foot--strike (hardware damage, hard landing) \\
Swing clearance& $h_f \ge h_{\min}$ ($0.1$ m, NIC3) &
  scuffing / premature re--contact (a ``step in place''); ensures $S$ is hit once, at touchdown \\
Dynamic constr.& EoM &
  (free here --- we integrate the ODE, so it holds by construction) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Values do not transfer from the reference.} The thesis numbers
($|u|\le 5$ Nm, $F_z\ge 200$ N, $F_e\le 15$ Ns) are for ATRIAS: \textbf{63 kg
with 50:1 harmonic drives}, so its $5$ Nm \emph{motor} torque is $250$ Nm at the
joint. RABBIT here is $\sim$30 kg with \textbf{no gear ratio} ($u$ is the joint
torque directly), so the limits must be re--derived --- measure first
(\fname{inspect\_solution} prints all four), then set bounds.

\paragraph{Order matters --- a worked diagnosis.} Measured on the reference
gait, the quantities were:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
quantity & measured & verdict \\
\midrule
peak $|u|$      & $257$ Nm      & high ($>u_{\max}$) \\
mean $F_z$      & $+309$ N      & $\approx$ body weight, correct sign \\
\textbf{min} $F_z$ & $\mathbf{-193}$ N & \warn{negative: the foot lifts off} \\
$\max|F_x/F_z|$ & $255$         & artifact of $F_z\!\to\!0$ (not real slip) \\
$|F_e|$         & $9.8$ Ns      & fine \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The instructive point: the enormous friction ratio is \emph{not} a friction
problem. $|F_x/F_z|$ diverges wherever $F_z$ crosses zero, and here $F_z$ goes
negative --- physically impossible, since the ground can only push. So the
\emph{ground--reaction} constraint ($F_z>0$, foot in compression) is the root;
the friction ratio only becomes meaningful once it holds (and
\fname{gait\_forces} therefore samples $|F_x/F_z|$ only where $F_z>1$ N).
Enabling friction first (before fixing $F_z$) made \code{fmincon} chase a
divide--by--zero and diverge; enabling \emph{GRF first} is correct. Each
constraint has its own toggle (\code{p.enforce\_grf}, \code{enforce\_friction},
\code{enforce\_torque}, \code{enforce\_impulse}) precisely so they can be
introduced one at a time, warm--started, in the physically sensible order.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_joint_torque.png}
\caption{A gait caught \emph{mid--way} through that one--at--a--time workflow.
The saved collocation result has \code{enforce\_grf} and \code{enforce\_impulse}
on but \code{enforce\_torque} still \textbf{off}, and the lower panel shows the
consequence: exactly one of the $80$ node torques leaves the $\pm120$~Nm box
(circled) --- the stance knee at the first node, at $-139.0$~Nm, a $16\%$
overshoot. With the toggle off this is not a solver failure but an
\emph{unconstrained} quantity, and it is precisely what enabling
\code{enforce\_torque} on the next warm--started solve is there to remove. The
remaining $79$ nodes sit well inside the box, which is why the violation is
easy to miss without plotting it.}
\label{fig:torque}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The constraints are coupled, not four independent knobs.} Two
couplings are worth internalizing. First, \emph{GRF is upstream of friction}:
the ratio $|F_x/F_z|$ diverges as $F_z\!\to\!0$, so friction is only well posed
once $F_z>0$ is enforced --- the ordering is dictated by the math, not taste.
Second, \emph{torque is upstream of almost everything}: a tighter torque budget
caps joint accelerations, which caps how hard the robot throws its mass, which
shrinks GRF spikes and friction demand. A violent gait therefore violates the
whole table at once --- which is exactly what the reference seed does ($257$ Nm,
$F_z$ changing sign, $\rho\!\approx\!129$). These are four views of one question:
\emph{is this gait gentle enough to be real?}

\paragraph{Caveat.} The reference gait violates almost every one of these by
large margins \emph{and} is strongly unstable ($\rho\gg1$). Repairing it
incrementally may not converge from such a pathological seed; a materially
better initial gait, or the reduced 2--D zero--dynamics formulation, is the
likely path to a gait that is simultaneously periodic, stable, and physically
realizable.

\paragraph{Synthesis: constraints as specification completeness.} Every
constraint beyond the raw equations of motion was added because the optimizer
found a loophole in an incomplete specification, and each loophole was a
\emph{different} flavour of ``technically periodic'':
\begin{enumerate}
  \item periodic but \textbf{degenerate} (step in place) $\rightarrow$ step--length floor;
  \item periodic but \textbf{unstable} (falls after two steps) $\rightarrow$ stability, $\rho<1$;
  \item periodic but \textbf{unphysical} (foot lifts, slips, over--torque) $\rightarrow$ Table~3.1.
\end{enumerate}
The optimizer was never wrong; it did exactly what it was told, and each
surprising result exposed a missing line in the specification. Table~3.1 is
best read not as ``extra realism'' bolted on, but as \emph{the rest of the
definition of walking} --- the part that is obvious to a human and invisible to
\code{fmincon} until it is written down.

\section{Reproducibility}
% =====================================================================
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Generated dynamics.} \code{Tt,T1--T4,P\_st,P\_sw,M,DM,V,G} come
        from \fname{rabbit\_energy\_model\_generalized\_Lagrange.m};
        \code{J\_st,J\_sw,Jdotdq\_st,Jdotdq\_sw} from \fname{Jacobians.m}.
        \emph{If any mass, length, inertia, or kinematic relation changes, these
        generator scripts must be re--run} to regenerate the committed files.
        Do not hand--edit the generated files.
  \item \textbf{Saved results.} \fname{Results/hzd\_result\_<timestamp>.mat}
        each store \code{z\_opt, p, fval, exitflag, report}. The
        \code{2026-07-20\_12-06-36} result is the reference periodic gait
        ($\approx 0.355\,$m/s) used as the warm--start seed.
        \fname{build\_gait\_library.m} writes
        \fname{gait\_library\_<timestamp>.mat}. Collocation solves
        (Section~\ref{sec:collocation}) write
        \fname{Results/col\_result\_<timestamp>.mat}, which additionally carry
        \code{N} --- that field is how \fname{rabbit\_hzd\_collocation.m} tells
        a collocation warm start from a PD one.
  \item \textbf{Default result selection is by filename, not mtime.} Every
        entry point that resolves a default \fname{Results/*.mat} ---
        \fname{rabbit\_hzd\_collocation}, \fname{col\_crosscheck},
        \fname{col\_poincare}, \fname{col\_verify\_seed},
        \fname{Test/test\_clf\_qp} --- picks
        \code{max} of \code{sort(\{d.name\})}, \emph{not} \code{max([d.datenum])}.
        The timestamp embedded in the filename is zero--padded, so a
        lexicographic sort is chronological, whereas filesystem mtime is not
        checkout--invariant: a fresh \code{git clone} or \code{git worktree add}
        stamps every checked--out file with the same time, and an mtime
        \code{max} then breaks the tie arbitrarily --- in practice returning the
        \emph{oldest} gait. Selecting by mtime therefore silently seeds a solve
        from a stale result and makes the outcome depend on which checkout it
        runs in.
  \item \textbf{Figures.} The result figures in this document
        (Figures~\ref{fig:stick}, \ref{fig:crosscheck}, \ref{fig:torque},
        \ref{fig:limitcycle}) are not hand--drawn: they are produced by
        \fname{Visualization/make\_doc\_figures.m} from the newest
        \fname{Results/col\_result\_*.mat} --- here
        \fname{col\_result\_2026-07-23\_00-45-04.mat} --- into
        \fname{docs/figures/}. After the reference gait changes, re--run that
        script and recompile (\code{pdflatex} twice, for the cross--references)
        so the figures and the prose cannot drift apart.
  \item \textbf{Nondeterminism.} \fname{make\_random\_initial\_state.m} uses
        \code{randn} with no seed. The HZD pipeline instead uses a hard--coded
        $x_0$, so its results are reproducible. Add \code{rng(seed)} before
        random sampling if determinism is needed there.
  \item \textbf{Orphaned cache.} \fname{Trajectory\_Optimization/Trajectory.mat}
        is committed but loaded by no code path; treat as unverified.
  \item \textbf{Simscape.} \fname{Dynamics/Test\_dynamics\_Simscape.slx} is a
        standalone dynamics cross--check, not part of the runtime pipeline.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known issues and broken legacy entry points}
\label{sec:known}
% =====================================================================
The following predate the HZD pipeline and \warn{do not run as--is} --- they
call functions that no longer exist. They are retained for reference only.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \fname{main\_demo.m}: calls \code{make\_initial\_state} expecting 3
        outputs (it returns 1), \code{simulate\_n\_steps} with 4 arguments (it
        takes 3), and \code{animate\_rabbit\_stepping\_stones} (does not exist).
  \item \fname{Controller/rabbit\_controller.m}: calls \code{desired\_gait} /
        \code{desired\_gait\_velocity} (do not exist).
  \item \fname{Dynamics/rabbit\_dynamics.m}: calls \code{D\_matrix} /
        \code{C\_vector} / \code{G\_vector} (do not exist; the real terms are
        \code{M} / \code{V} / \code{G}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open item: stance--foot constraint drift (a symptom).}
The stance foot is held only by the acceleration--level contact constraint, so
\code{ode45} permits numerical drift of the foot within a step
($\sim 0.1$--$0.3$~m; \fname{inspect\_solution.m} reports it). The natural fix
is Baumgarte stabilization (\fname{rabbit\_constrained\_dynamics.m} supports it
via an optional \code{foot\_ref}), but on the reference gait it \emph{diverged}
rather than helping. That is not a flaw in Baumgarte: as
Section~\ref{sec:stability} shows, the seed orbit has $\rho\approx 3$, so it
amplifies the very corrections Baumgarte injects. The drift is thus best read as
\emph{another symptom of orbital instability}; enforcing $\rho<1$ addresses the
root cause, after which constraint stabilization becomes well behaved.

\paragraph{Open item: enforcing stability is expensive.}
The $\rho<1$ constraint is finite--difference heavy (Section~\ref{sec:stability}).
It is usable as a warm--started final phase but slow for a from--scratch solve; a
reduced (2--dimensional hybrid zero dynamics) stability condition, or an
analytic Poincar\'e Jacobian, would make it cheap. This is the natural next step
for the pipeline, alongside the CLF--QP controller route (which stabilizes an
open--loop--unstable orbit with feedback instead of redesigning it).
