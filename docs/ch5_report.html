§ 5.0  ·  the claim

One idea, and a mechanism that has to survive it

Given a constraint function h(x), the safe set is C = {x : h(x) ≥ 0}, and the goal is forward invariance: start in C, stay in C, forever. Not "track a reference that happens to stay inside," and not "penalize leaving" — stay in it.

The mechanism is the one that made the control Lyapunov function useful in Chapter 3: find a condition that is affine in the control, so a quadratic program can carry it as a row. Section 5.1 finds one. It just does not work here — and the reason it does not is the whole motivation for the chapter.

Chapter 5 deliberately does not use RABBIT

The chapter validates the construction on two purpose-built plants — a relative-degree-6 serial spring-mass chain and a relative-degree-4 two-link pendulum with elastic actuators — and only then, "in the next Chapter," applies it to walking. So Chapter5/ carries its own two models and shares nothing with Chapters 3 and 4 but the code style.

§ 5.1  ·  the failure that motivates everything

Reciprocal barriers, and the row with no control in it

The reciprocal candidate (5.6) is B = 1/h, blowing up at the boundary, under condition (5.3) Ḃ ≤ γ/B. Substituting B = 1/h, so Ḃ = −ḣ/h² and γ/B = γh, gives the far more readable form the implementation actually uses:

ḣ ≥ −γ h³ and ḣ = L_f h + L_g h · u

So the row constrains u only through L_g h. For a constraint of relative degree r_b > 1, L_g h ≡ 0 — that is the definition of relative degree — and the barrier row degenerates to a statement with no control in it at all.

Two outcomes follow, and ch5_ctrl_cbf_clf_qp distinguishes them rather than lumping both into "it failed": where the right-hand side is ≤ 0 the row is vacuously true — the QP solves and quietly returns the plain CLF-QP control, which is the failure that hides until the state crosses the boundary. Where it turns > 0 the row is unsatisfiable and the QP is infeasible. On the spring-mass, both appear, in that order.

The controlled comparison: one plant, two relative degrees

Holding the plant and the initial condition fixed and varying only the relative degree is what makes this evidence rather than anecdote. If the reciprocal CBF had failed on a different system, one could blame the system.

A second limitation, less often stated

B = 1/h is a barrier only on the interior {h > 0}. At h ≤ 0 the reciprocal is negative or infinite and the sandwich between class-𝒦 functions has already failed, so a reciprocal CBF has no recovery behaviour if the state is ever perturbed out of the set. An ECBF condition is a polynomial in the derivatives of h and stays perfectly well defined at h < 0. Both facts are checked directly: at h = −1.00, 1/h is undefined in 2 of 2 constructions while the ECBF row still acts.

§ 5.2  ·  the construction

Exponential CBFs: from a control law to a constraint

Virtual input–output linearization

Differentiate h all the way to r_b and apply the (5.21) split. What comes out is affine in μ — one scalar row, exactly like the CLF row — and nothing about the plant survives into it:

h⁽ʳᵇ⁾ = L_f^rᵇ h + L_g L_f^(rᵇ−1) h · u = L_f^rᵇ h + L_g L_f^(rᵇ−1) h · (u_ff + A⁻¹ μ) =: b₀(x) + L_b(x) μ =: μ_b (VIOL) barrier dynamics — a chain of r_b integrators, eq (5.23) η̇_b = F_b η_b + G_b μ_b, η_b = [h; ḣ; …; h⁽ʳᵇ⁻¹⁾]

The step from driving to bounding

This is the whole construction, and it is one character wide.

The conditions, and which ones bite

Four conditions come with the theorem. Two are structural and are enforced at construction; two depend on the initial condition and are checked at the real x₀ before anything is integrated.

Total negative is not pedantry. Theorem 5.1 is proved by applying Proposition 5.1 r_b times down the chain C_rb → … → C_0, and each link uses the recursion y_i = ẏ_{i−1} + p_i y_{i−1} with p_i a real positive number. A complex conjugate pair is perfectly Hurwitz and produces the right characteristic polynomial, but there is no real p_i to build the intermediate sets from, so the induction has no rungs. The test suite checks that complex poles are refused, and that eig(F_b − G_b K_b) == −poles exactly — not merely that the result is stable. (K_b = fliplr(poly(−p)) and K_b = poly(−p) are both Hurwitz; only one places the poles you asked for.)

Corollary 5.2 depends on x₀, which the chapter itself flags as a real limitation. Read the rule for what it is: if the barrier is already heading toward the boundary at x₀, the poles must be fast enough to have arrested it in time. Slow poles are not a conservative choice here — they are an inadmissible one. The gate runs before integration, because running from an inadmissible x₀ produces a controller that enforces its row faithfully at every instant and still lets h go negative, which looks exactly like a bug in the barrier and is not one.

The quadratic program

min_{μ,δ} μᵀμ + p δ² (5.31) s.t. V̇(η,μ) + λV(η) ≤ δ CLF — slacked A_c(x) μ ≤ b_c(x) input box — off here μ_b ≥ −K_b η_b ECBF — HARD h⁽ʳᵇ⁾(x,μ) = μ_b VIOL

The barrier row is never slacked

Only the CLF row gets δ. When the two conflict — and on the pendulum they genuinely do — safety wins and tracking waits. A safety constraint you are willing to violate for a smoother input is not a safety constraint. That asymmetry shows up in the data: on the tighter pendulum run the CLF slack reaches |δ| = 56.5 while the barrier row is violated on zero samples.

By Remark 5.6 the ECBF row is exactly y_rb(x) ≥ 0 for the family (5.28), so qp.y_rb lets a run be checked against Theorem 5.1's chain of sets rather than only against h ≥ 0. That identity is asserted from two independent constructions and holds to 0.0e+00 on the pendulum and 2.2e−16 on the spring-mass.

§ 5.2.3  ·  the validation systems

Two plants, chosen for what they are not

Between them the two plants cover what the construction claims: one linear and one nonlinear, one single-input and one multi-input, at two different relative degrees. In both, the constrained quantity is the controlled quantity, so the safety constraint inherits the full relative degree of the plant. There is no cheap reformulation in which the barrier is relative degree 1 — which is exactly the situation Section 5.1 cannot address.

The pendulum maneuver is not a small one

θ₁: −π → +π with θ₂ held at 0. Both endpoints are the arm pointing straight up, and they differ by a full turn. The only way through is θ₁ = 0, where an unfolded arm puts the end effector at p₂ʸ = −2 m. So the constraint is not a mild bound near the trajectory — it is incompatible with θ₂ = 0 over part of the maneuver and forces the arm to fold against what the CLF is asking for. That conflict is what Fig. 5.4's "aggressively move the links" describes, and it is why the CLF row needs a slack while the barrier row must not have one.

Results II  ·  relative degree 4

Two-link pendulum with elastic actuators

The nonlinear case, and the one where the CLF and the barrier genuinely disagree. The series-elastic block is what makes the system relative degree 4 rather than 2: torque reaches the link only through the spring, i.e. through two extra integrators per joint. At k = 100 Nm/rad with J_m = 0.1 the motor–spring mode sits at √(k/J_m) = 31.6 rad/s — comfortably above the arm's own ~3 rad/s and comfortably below the 2 kHz control rate, a real SEA regime rather than a stiff limit that would collapse back to relative degree 2.

The barrier is active for only a few percent of the run, which is the interesting part. The poles [5 5.5 6 10] are fast relative to the arm, so the row starts pushing well before h reaches zero: the end effector approaches the limit and turns away rather than sliding along it. The exponential envelope of Definition 5.1 is doing exactly what it is for. The barrier's active window widens from 0.49 – 3.48 s at p₂ₘᵢₙ = −1.0 to 0.49 – 11.19 s at −0.5 — the tighter bound keeps the row engaged for most of the maneuver.

The cost is visible in θ₂. The CLF wants θ₂ → 0 throughout, and in the ECBF runs θ₂ is driven to −2.23 rad and −3.10 rad mid-maneuver — the arm folding. That is the hard barrier row winning against the slacked CLF row. Note what it does not cost: both ECBF runs finish the rotation more accurately than the baseline does (0.0013 and 0.0152 rad against 0.0544), and both do it at a lower 99th-percentile torque.

Caveats  ·  measured, not assumed

Three things worth being explicit about

1. The trajectory is not reproducible; the safety property is

Once the barrier row is active this closed loop is a discrete-time sliding mode — the state leaves the boundary between samples and is pushed back at the next one — and which side of an active-set switch a sample lands on is decided far below the integration error. Measured directly:

Integrator dependencech5_test_ecbf · rk4 vs ode45, identical x₀
Plantmin h (rk4)min h (ode45)Trajectory separationSafety verdict
Spring-mass (linear)+1.26e+00+1.26e+003.2e−14Agrees

On the pendulum the two integrators separate by ~0.5 rad within 3 s while agreeing completely on the thing that is claimed. On the linear plant they agree to 1e−14.

So no claim here rests on an individual pendulum trajectory, and the figures above should be read as one realization. What both integrators agree on — and what the test suite asserts for both — is that the safe set is never left. The pendulum therefore defaults to fixed-step rk4: ode45's adaptive stepping buys no fidelity in a quantity that is not reproducible anyway.

2. Sampled-data enforcement is only O(dt)

Forward invariance is a continuous-time claim; the row is imposed at sample instants and the plant moves between them. Measured on the pendulum at p₂ₘᵢₙ = −1.0:

The default is 0.5 ms — the first rate at which the reproduction actually exhibits invariance rather than nearly does. ch5_report tags an excursion as safe~O(dt) only when it is within 10·dt, so a real violation cannot hide behind the label.

3. The min-norm torque spike

A min-norm CLF-QP applies ‖μ‖ = ψ/‖L_gV‖, which is unbounded wherever L_gV passes near zero. On the pendulum baseline this happens once, at t = 0.224 s, where the torque touches 435 Nm for one sample out of 30 001 against a 99th percentile of 26.6 Nm. This is a property of the min-norm formulation, not of the barrier — the ECBF runs show no such sample. The report prints max|u| beside p99|u| and names the spike; the figures use a robust shared axis and annotate the true peak rather than compressing every curve to accommodate it or cropping it silently.

A solver note that is not marginal

quadprog runs active-set here, not the interior-point-convex that Chapters 3 and 4 use. When the barrier row is pushing hard, the CLF objective and the ECBF constraint disagree by a wide margin — the CLF alone would use ‖μ‖ ~ 8 where the barrier row demands ~93. As recorded in the repository's own investigation notes, interior-point failed on 222 of 30 001 samples with exitflag −3 ("unbounded") on a problem whose Hessian is positive definite and therefore cannot be unbounded; re-solving those same QPs, active-set succeeded on 222/222 with worst residual 1.4e−11. Two supporting fixes came out of the same investigation: constraint rows are normalized by their coefficients only, never including the right-hand side (ch5_scale_row), and every QP is warm-started at the closed-form min-norm CLF control (ch5_min_norm_mu).

Two checks are worth calling out because they guard against silent wrongness rather than loud wrongness. eig(F_b − G_b K_b) == −poles catches the reversed pole-gain, which is Hurwitz either way and would place the wrong poles without complaint. And the barrier's fourth derivatives are validated two independent ways — against finite differences (loose, but sensitive to a wrong derivative) and against a hand-written Faà di Bruno chain rule (exact, to 1.4e−15) — because nested finite differences lose ~3 digits per level, and by level 4 the error is O(1e−3) on a quantity the controller sits exactly on.

§ 5.3  ·  what carries forward

What this hands to Chapter 6

An ECBF is a barrier row that is affine in μ, works at any relative degree, is designed by pole placement, and sits in the same QP as the CLF. Chapter 6 applies exactly that to walking with varied step length and width, and to time-varying stepping stones — where Section 6.1.1's "modification of CBF for position based constraints" turns out to be the relative-degree-2 case of this construction. That identity is not an analogy: ch6_test_barrier asserts the two rows are numerically identical, and ch5_test_ecbf checks the same thing from its own side (Remark 5.7, err 1.1e−16).

Two limitations to carry with it

  • Pole choice depends on the initial condition. Corollary 5.2 ties admissible poles to x₀, so a pole set that is valid for one start is not automatically valid for another. On a walking robot, where x₀ is whatever the previous step's impact map produced, this is a live constraint rather than a formality. ch5_ecbf_admissible is the gate; the chapter itself flags the dependence as a real limitation of the method.
  • The whole construction is model-based. Every term in the row — L_f^rb h, L_g L_f^(rb−1) h, the decoupling matrix — comes from the model. That is precisely the gap Chapter 4 spent its length on, and nothing in Chapter 5 addresses it. A robust or adaptive ECBF is the obvious composition and is not attempted here.
  • Both validation plants have no zero dynamics. r·n_y = n_x on both, so η is a change of coordinates on the whole state and nothing can hide in an internal mode. That is a convenience of these systems that Chapter 6's walking robot will not share.

ch5_main is resumable and checkpoints after every study; only selects individual runs within a study, and anything already in the file is never redone. Dependencies: the Optimization Toolbox for quadprog, and the Symbolic Math Toolbox only to regenerate Model/Generated. No Statistics Toolbox — ch5_prctile exists so that printing a table does not add a third dependency.

On the generated code

The ECBF condition needs L_f^rb h and L_g L_f^(rb−1) h exactly. For r_b = 4 on a two-link arm those are fourth derivatives of a trigonometric expression through an inverted mass matrix, and both obvious alternatives are bad: nested finite differences lose ~3 digits per level, and complex-step is exact but does not nest. So they are derived symbolically once and committed — the same convention the repository already uses for Dynamics/. ch5_gen_pendulum calls ch5_pendulum with syms in place of numbers, so there is exactly one statement of the dynamics in the repository and the generated derivatives cannot drift from the model the simulation integrates.
